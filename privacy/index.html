Privacy policy for the Satis calorie app

Your food log stays on your iPhone and in your own iCloud. In the App Store version only a few things can leave it, each only when you use that feature, and never your diary.

The short version. Your food log, weight and settings live on your iPhone and, if you turn on sync, in your own iCloud. We can't see them. A few things can leave your phone, each only when you use the feature: a phrase the food database can't read, sent as text to Google Gemini through our server if you turn AI reading on; a barcode number, sent to Open Food Facts and to our server; the text of a nutrition label (never the photo), sent to Google Gemini through our server when the phone isn't sure of the numbers; a food you choose to share with other Satis users; and what you allow Apple Health to receive. If you subscribe to Satis Pro, a random purchase ID and your subscription status go to RevenueCat, which manages subscriptions for us. TestFlight beta builds add one more, only if you say yes: anonymous usage counts, with no food, weight or health values in them. We never sell data and there are no ads.

What the app stores, and where

To work, Satis keeps:

  • the food you log, your recipes, your usual meals and any portion sizes you calibrate (your katori, your roti);
  • your weigh-ins and, if you add them, progress photos;
  • the answers you give when setting up (age, height, weight, the formula used for your starting estimate, activity, pace, goal, and the safety questions, such as pregnancy) and your settings;
  • your answers to the beta's "How was today?" card, if you use it.

All of this is stored on your iPhone. If you turn on iCloud sync, it also syncs through Apple's CloudKit private database in your own iCloud account, so it appears on your other devices. Only you can read that private database; we have no access to it. Apple's privacy policy covers iCloud. Widgets and the Apple Watch app read the same data on your own devices. Photos for "snap now, log later", photos of a nutrition label and progress photos stay on your device and in your iCloud: a label is read on the phone, and no photo is sent to an AI service or to us. If the phone isn't sure of a label's numbers, only the text it read can be sent for a second read (section 2b).

What leaves your phone, who receives it, and why

1. Phrases the database can't match (AI reading, off until you agree)

When you type or say a meal, Satis first matches it against the food database on your phone. If part of the sentence can't be matched and you have turned AI reading on, only that unmatched text (for example "1 plate mutton nihari") is sent to be read as food names and amounts. Satis asks for your permission the first time, and you can turn it off any time in Settings → AI.

  • The route. The text goes to our server, a small program run on Cloudflare Workers, with a random ID made for your install, which is used only to limit how many requests one phone can make. Our server passes only the text to the Google Gemini API, on a paid Google Cloud account. Your weight, your profile, your history and your Apple ID are never sent.
  • Who processes it. Google (Gemini API) and Cloudflare act as our processors under their data processing terms. On paid Gemini services, Google does not use the text to train its models; it may keep it for a limited time to detect abuse, under the Gemini API terms. Our server keeps no logs.
  • The numbers. Calories and nutrients always come from Satis's own database on your phone. The AI only reads names and amounts.
  • Why we ask first. A meal description can reveal something about your health or beliefs, which the UK GDPR and GDPR treat as special category data. We rely on your explicit consent, which you can withdraw in Settings → AI; withdrawing doesn't affect what was read before.
  • In the beta. Developer builds can send the text straight to the Gemini API with a key the developer or tester adds; App Store builds always use our server. Please don't type names or other personal details into a meal.

2. Barcode lookups (Open Food Facts, USDA and foods shared by Satis users)

When you scan a packaged food, the barcode number is sent to Open Food Facts, an open, non-profit food database, and to our server on Cloudflare Workers, which checks the foods Satis users have shared (section 2c) and a copy of the US Department of Agriculture's public FoodData Central branded foods list. Nothing else is sent with it except, to our server, the random ID made for your install, used only to limit how many requests one phone can make. Like any website, Open Food Facts can see the internet address of the request and handles it under its own privacy policy. Our server keeps no record of lookups. We do this to give you the food you asked for (performance of our contract with you). A product you scan is saved on your phone so the next scan works offline. Satis doesn't send corrections to Open Food Facts.

2b. A second read of a nutrition label (text only)

When a barcode isn't found, you can photograph the pack's nutrition table. Satis reads the photo on your phone. If it isn't sure of some numbers, it can send the text it read (for example "Energy (kcal) 512, Protein (g) 6.5") to our server, which passes only that text to the Google Gemini API, on the same paid account as AI reading, to read the table a second time. The photo is never sent. The first time, Satis asks you to tap "Read it again with Satis AI"; after that it does it by itself. The answer is shown for you to check before anything is saved, and numbers Gemini returns that aren't in the label's text are thrown away. Our server keeps no record of the text. Google and Cloudflare act as our processors, as in section 1. We do this because you asked Satis to read the label (performance of our contract with you).

2c. Foods you share with other Satis users (only if you leave sharing on)

When you save a food from a pack's label for a barcode, a switch, "Share with Satis users", is on by default and can be turned off for that food before you save. If it's on, Satis sends our server the barcode, the food's name and brand, its calories, protein, carbohydrate, fat and fibre per 100 g, its serving size, and whether you read it from a photo or typed it. Other people who scan that barcode then get those values, marked "Community", with a check once two or more people's readings agree. Nothing about you is shown to them.

  • What our server keeps. The food values above, the day (not the time) they were shared, and a salted, one-way hash of your install's random ID, used only so that one install counts once per barcode and to cap how many foods one install can share a day. We don't keep your internet address, your Apple ID or anything from your diary, and we can't tell who you are from the hash.
  • Checks. Values that are impossible (for example more calories than fat can give, or more than 100 g of nutrients in 100 g) are refused.
  • Where it is stored. In a Cloudflare D1 database run for us by Cloudflare, our processor.
  • Our legal basis is your consent, given by leaving the switch on for that food. Deleting the food in Satis withdraws your shared reading from our server. By sharing, you let Satis show the values to other Satis users and keep them in its food database.

3. Apple Health (with your permission)

If you connect Apple Health, Satis can write the food you log (energy, protein, carbohydrate, fat, fibre and caffeine) and your weigh-ins, and can read your body weight and active energy. You choose each permission in the Health app and can change it any time. This stays between Satis and Health on your devices. Health data is used only to run Satis's features. It is never used for advertising, never sold, and never shared with anyone.

3b. Health Connect on Android (with your permission)

On Android, if you connect Health Connect, Satis can write the nutrition you log and your weigh-ins, and can read your body weight. You choose each permission in Health Connect and can change it any time. This data stays between Satis and Health Connect on your phone. Satis's use of data from Health Connect follows the Health Connect Permissions policy, including its Limited Use requirements: it is used only to run Satis's features, never sold, never used for advertising, and never shared with anyone else.

Reading text from photos on Android. When you scan a nutrition label or a food-order screenshot, Satis reads the text on your phone with Google's ML Kit. The photo and its text stay on the phone. ML Kit may send Google anonymous diagnostics about how the feature performs, such as its version and speed, as described in Google's ML Kit terms.

4. Beta usage counts (TestFlight builds only, if you say yes)

Beta builds ask once, on first open, whether you'll share anonymous usage counts to help us fix the beta. If you tap Share:

  • What is sent. Events such as "app opened", "a meal was logged" (with how it was logged, such as typed or scanned, and a rough item count like 2–3), "a day was logged", "a weigh-in was saved", "the weekly check-in was opened", which onboarding screen number was reached, and your "How was today?" answers. Each event carries the app version, iOS major version and a random ID made for your install.
  • What is never sent. Food names, amounts, calories, weights, body measurements, Apple Health values, notes, photos, your Apple ID or a device identifier. Onboarding screens that only appear because of a health or safety answer send nothing.
  • Who receives it. PostHog, our analytics processor, in its US cloud region. We ask PostHog not to look up location and not to store your internet address, and we don't build person profiles.
  • Turning it off. Settings → Privacy → "Share anonymous usage". Turning it off stops sending and deletes the random ID and any events still waiting on your phone; if you turn it back on, you appear as a new anonymous install.
  • App Store builds never send analytics, whatever you chose in the beta. Our legal basis is your consent.

Things you send yourself

  • Feedback. When you shake your phone or tap Send feedback, Satis prepares a note with a screenshot and, if you choose, your recent log entries and check-ins, and opens the share sheet. Nothing is sent unless you pick where it goes, for example an email to us.
  • Voice. Speaking a meal uses Apple's speech recognition, on the device where your iPhone supports it. Apple's privacy policy covers it.
  • TestFlight. In the beta, Apple shares crash reports and any feedback you submit through TestFlight with us, under Apple's TestFlight terms.
  • Purchases. Apple (on iPhone) or Google Play (on Android) handles payment for Satis Pro; we never see your card or payment details. RevenueCat, our subscription processor, receives a random ID made for your install plus the receipt Apple or Google Play issues (product, price, dates, renewal and trial status), so Satis can check whether Pro is active and the founder can grant free time under the 4-week promise. It is not linked to your name, email or Apple ID, and it is not used for ads or tracking. If you email us to claim the 4-week promise, the email includes that random ID so we can find your subscription.

What we never do

Satis has no accounts and no advertising. We don't track you across other companies' apps or websites, and we don't sell, rent or share personal data for advertising. Consumer health data is covered in more detail on our consumer health data privacy policy.

This website

  • Beta sign-up. The site has no sign-up form. Joining the beta happens in Apple's TestFlight app, under Apple's privacy policy. If you email us to join the Android waitlist, we keep your email only to tell you when Android is ready.
  • Hosting. Our host keeps standard server logs, such as internet address and pages requested, for a short time to keep the site running and secure (our legitimate interest).
  • Cookies. The site sets no cookies. If you pick light or dark, or a colour, that choice is saved in your browser's local storage and never sent anywhere. The real-burn tool keeps weigh-ins you paste in this tab's session storage so a reload doesn't lose them; the day builder keeps your day in local storage. Neither is sent anywhere, and closing the tab (or Clear) removes the weigh-ins.

Website analytics (cookieless)

We count visits to this website with PostHog, so we can see which pages and tools help people and fix the ones that don't. It is set up to need no cookie banner:

  • No cookies or storage. Nothing is saved in your browser for analytics. To count a visit across pages, PostHog's server combines your internet address and browser type with a secret that changes every day and keeps only the resulting code, so visits can't be linked from one day to the next or to you.
  • What is recorded. The page you view and its type (home, food, guide, tool, legal), the page you came from, any campaign tags in the link (utm_source and the like), your browser, device type and rough location (country and city, from your internet address), and a few named actions: tapping a beta or App Store button, opening TestFlight, starting or finishing a tool, sharing a result, opening a shared recipe, reading half or most of a guide, and following a link to another site.
  • What is never recorded. Anything you type into the tools (your age, weight, weigh-ins or food), the answers or recipes held in a page's link, the text on the page, mouse movements or screen recordings. We strip everything after the ? and # from our own addresses before sending, except campaign tags.
  • Your choice. If your browser sends Do Not Track or Global Privacy Control, the analytics never load. Content blockers that block PostHog work too.
  • Who and where. PostHog, our analytics processor, in its US cloud region. Visits are kept up to 12 months. Our legal basis is our legitimate interest in understanding, in aggregate, how the site is used.

International transfers

Satis is run from India, and our processors are mainly in the United States. When personal data from the UK or the EU goes to them:

  • Google and Cloudflare: under the EU–US Data Privacy Framework and its UK Extension, where the company is certified, and otherwise under the European Commission's standard contractual clauses with the UK Addendum, as set out in their data processing terms.
  • RevenueCat: under its data processing agreement and the European Commission's standard contractual clauses.
  • PostHog (beta usage counts and website analytics): stored in its US region, under its data processing agreement and the European Commission's standard contractual clauses.
  • Data you send us directly (the beta form, emails, feedback) is received by us in India. UK and EU data protection law still applies to it, and you keep the rights below.

Deleting your data

  • Deleting Satis from your iPhone removes its data from that device.
  • To remove the synced copy, go to Settings → [your name] → iCloud → Manage Account Storage → Satis.
  • Data Satis wrote to Apple Health stays there until you delete it in the Health app (Browse → Nutrition or Body Measurements).
  • Deleting a food you shared, in Satis, removes your shared reading from our server.
  • For the beta list, feedback or emails, write to us and we'll delete your details within 30 days.

Your rights

Depending on where you live, you can ask us to:

  • confirm whether we hold personal data about you, and give you a copy;
  • correct it, delete it, or limit how we use it;
  • give it to you in a portable format;
  • stop using it where we rely on our legitimate interests;
  • withdraw a consent you gave (AI reading in Settings → AI; beta usage counts in Settings → Privacy; a shared food by deleting it in Satis), without affecting what happened before.

These rights come from the UK GDPR, the EU GDPR, India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023, and US state laws such as California's and Washington's. Because the app's data sits on your phone and in your own iCloud, you already hold most of it. For anything we hold, email shaharechaitanya3@gmail.com; we reply within one month. Beta usage counts are tied only to a random ID we can't link to you, so we can only find them if you send us that ID.

You can complain to a data protection authority: in the UK, the Information Commissioner's Office; in the EU, the authority where you live; in India, the Data Protection Board of India. We'd like the chance to fix it first, so please write to us.

Children

Satis is for adults aged 18 and over. We don't knowingly collect data from anyone younger. If you think a child has joined the beta list, tell us and we'll remove them.

Security

Your data is protected by your iPhone's encryption and by iCloud. Connections to our server, Google, Open Food Facts and PostHog use HTTPS. A Gemini key added in a developer build is kept in the iOS Keychain.

Changes

If this policy changes, we'll update the date above. For material changes we'll tell you in the app, or by email to beta testers, before the change applies.
