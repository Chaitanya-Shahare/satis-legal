Privacy policy for the Satis calorie app

Your food log stays on your iPhone and in your own iCloud. In the App Store version only three things can leave it, each when you use that feature.

The short version. Your food log, weight and settings live on your iPhone and, if you turn on sync, in your own iCloud. We can't see them. Three things can leave your phone, each only when you use the feature: a phrase the food database can't read, sent as text to Google Gemini through our server if you turn AI reading on; a barcode number, sent to Open Food Facts; and what you allow Apple Health to receive. TestFlight beta builds add a fourth, only if you say yes: anonymous usage counts, with no food, weight or health values in them. We never sell data and there are no ads.

What the app stores, and where

To work, Satis keeps:

  • the food you log, your recipes, your usual meals and any portion sizes you calibrate (your katori, your roti);
  • your weigh-ins and, if you add them, progress photos;
  • the answers you give when setting up (age, height, weight, the formula used for your starting estimate, activity, pace, goal, and the safety questions, such as pregnancy) and your settings;
  • your answers to the beta's "How was today?" card, if you use it.

All of this is stored on your iPhone. If you turn on iCloud sync, it also syncs through Apple's CloudKit private database in your own iCloud account, so it appears on your other devices. Only you can read that private database; we have no access to it. Apple's privacy policy covers iCloud. Widgets and the Apple Watch app read the same data on your own devices. Photos for "snap now, log later", photos of a nutrition label and progress photos stay on your device and in your iCloud: a label is read on the phone, and no photo is sent to an AI service or to us.

What leaves your phone, who receives it, and why

1. Phrases the database can't match (AI reading, off until you agree)

When you type or say a meal, Satis first matches it against the food database on your phone. If part of the sentence can't be matched and you have turned AI reading on, only that unmatched text (for example "1 plate mutton nihari") is sent to be read as food names and amounts. Satis asks for your permission the first time, and you can turn it off any time in Settings → AI.

  • The route. The text goes to our server, a small program run on Cloudflare Workers, with a random ID made for your install, which is used only to limit how many requests one phone can make. Our server passes only the text to the Google Gemini API, on a paid Google Cloud account. Your weight, your profile, your history and your Apple ID are never sent.
  • Who processes it. Google (Gemini API) and Cloudflare act as our processors under their data processing terms. On paid Gemini services, Google does not use the text to train its models; it may keep it for a limited time to detect abuse, under the Gemini API terms. Our server keeps no logs.
  • The numbers. Calories and nutrients always come from Satis's own database on your phone. The AI only reads names and amounts.
  • Why we ask first. A meal description can reveal something about your health or beliefs, which the UK GDPR and GDPR treat as special category data. We rely on your explicit consent, which you can withdraw in Settings → AI; withdrawing doesn't affect what was read before.
  • In the beta. Developer builds can send the text straight to the Gemini API with a key the developer or tester adds; App Store builds always use our server. Please don't type names or other personal details into a meal.

2. Barcode lookups (Open Food Facts)

When you scan a packaged food, the barcode number is sent to Open Food Facts, an open, non-profit food database, to fetch the product's label. Nothing else is sent with it, though, like any website, Open Food Facts can see the internet address of the request and handles it under its own privacy policy. We do this to give you the food you asked for (performance of our contract with you). If you then correct or add a product, it is saved on your phone; Satis doesn't send it to Open Food Facts unless a future version asks you first.

3. Apple Health (with your permission)

If you connect Apple Health, Satis can write the food you log (energy, protein, carbohydrate, fat, fibre and caffeine) and your weigh-ins, and can read your body weight and active energy. You choose each permission in the Health app and can change it any time. This stays between Satis and Health on your devices. Health data is used only to run Satis's features. It is never used for advertising, never sold, and never shared with anyone.

4. Beta usage counts (TestFlight builds only, if you say yes)

Beta builds ask once, on first open, whether you'll share anonymous usage counts to help us fix the beta. If you tap Share:

  • What is sent. Events such as "app opened", "a meal was logged" (with how it was logged, such as typed or scanned, and a rough item count like 2–3), "a day was logged", "a weigh-in was saved", "the weekly check-in was opened", which onboarding screen number was reached, and your "How was today?" answers. Each event carries the app version, iOS major version and a random ID made for your install.
  • What is never sent. Food names, amounts, calories, weights, body measurements, Apple Health values, notes, photos, your Apple ID or a device identifier. Onboarding screens that only appear because of a health or safety answer send nothing.
  • Who receives it. PostHog, our analytics processor, in its EU cloud region. We ask PostHog not to look up location and not to store your internet address, and we don't build person profiles.
  • Turning it off. Settings → Privacy → "Share anonymous usage". Turning it off stops sending and deletes the random ID and any events still waiting on your phone; if you turn it back on, you appear as a new anonymous install.
  • App Store builds never send analytics, whatever you chose in the beta. Our legal basis is your consent.

Things you send yourself

  • Feedback. When you shake your phone or tap Send feedback, Satis prepares a note with a screenshot and, if you choose, your recent log entries and check-ins, and opens the share sheet. Nothing is sent unless you pick where it goes, for example an email to us.
  • Voice. Speaking a meal uses Apple's speech recognition, on the device where your iPhone supports it. Apple's privacy policy covers it.
  • TestFlight. In the beta, Apple shares crash reports and any feedback you submit through TestFlight with us, under Apple's TestFlight terms.
  • Purchases. Once Satis has subscriptions, Apple handles them. We never see your payment details.

What we never do

Satis has no accounts and no advertising. We don't track you across other companies' apps or websites, and we don't sell, rent or share personal data for advertising. Consumer health data is covered in more detail on our consumer health data privacy policy.

International transfers

Satis is run from India, and our processors are mainly in the United States. When personal data from the UK or the EU goes to them:

  • Google and Cloudflare: under the EU–US Data Privacy Framework and its UK Extension, where the company is certified, and otherwise under the European Commission's standard contractual clauses with the UK Addendum, as set out in their data processing terms.
  • PostHog: stored in its EU region, under its data processing agreement and standard contractual clauses for any access from outside it.
  • Data you send us directly (the beta form, emails, feedback) is received by us in India. UK and EU data protection law still applies to it, and you keep the rights below.

Deleting your data

  • Deleting Satis from your iPhone removes its data from that device.
  • To remove the synced copy, go to Settings → [your name] → iCloud → Manage Account Storage → Satis.
  • Data Satis wrote to Apple Health stays there until you delete it in the Health app (Browse → Nutrition or Body Measurements).
  • For the beta list, feedback or emails, write to us and we'll delete your details within 30 days.

Your rights

Depending on where you live, you can ask us to:

  • confirm whether we hold personal data about you, and give you a copy;
  • correct it, delete it, or limit how we use it;
  • give it to you in a portable format;
  • stop using it where we rely on our legitimate interests;
  • withdraw a consent you gave (AI reading in Settings → AI; beta usage counts in Settings → Privacy), without affecting what happened before.

These rights come from the UK GDPR, the EU GDPR, India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023, and US state laws such as California's and Washington's. Because the app's data sits on your phone and in your own iCloud, you already hold most of it. For anything we hold, email shaharechaitanya3@gmail.com; we reply within one month. Beta usage counts are tied only to a random ID we can't link to you, so we can only find them if you send us that ID.

You can complain to a data protection authority: in the UK, the Information Commissioner's Office; in the EU, the authority where you live; in India, the Data Protection Board of India. We'd like the chance to fix it first, so please write to us.

Children

Satis is for adults aged 18 and over. We don't knowingly collect data from anyone younger. If you think a child has joined the beta list, tell us and we'll remove them.

Security

Your data is protected by your iPhone's encryption and by iCloud. Connections to our server, Google, Open Food Facts and PostHog use HTTPS. A Gemini key added in a developer build is kept in the iOS Keychain.

Changes

If this policy changes, we'll update the date above. For material changes we'll tell you in the app, or by email to beta testers, before the change applies.
