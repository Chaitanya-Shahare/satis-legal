Consumer health data privacy policy for the Satis calorie app

What you log in Satis can be health data. Almost all of it stays on your iPhone and in your own iCloud, and we never sell it.

Last updated 22 November 2026

Join the betaRead the page

In short. Satis keeps your food log, weight and goals on your device. We collect consumer health data only when you turn on a feature that needs it, and only with your consent: a meal phrase the app can't read (AI reading), and, in beta builds, anonymous usage counts. We never sell consumer health data, never use it for ads, and never geofence health care locations. This page is our Consumer Health Data Privacy Policy under Washington's My Health My Data Act. It adds to our privacy policy.

Consumer health data in Satis

Washington law treats information linked to you that describes your physical health, such as body weight, diet or eating-disorder history, as consumer health data. In Satis, that can include:

  • food and drink you log, meal descriptions you type or say, recipes and portion sizes;
  • body weight, weigh-ins, height, age, sex used for the calorie formula, activity level, goal and pace;
  • answers to the setup safety questions, such as pregnancy or a history of an eating disorder, and whether you use the gentler no-numbers mode;
  • progress photos;
  • body weight and active energy read from Apple Health, and nutrition and weight written to it;
  • your "How was today?" answers in the beta.

Where it comes from: you, when you use the app, and Apple Health on your device if you connect it.

Where it stays: on your iPhone, your Apple Watch and widgets, and, if you turn on iCloud sync, in your own iCloud private database. We can't read any of this, so for most of it we never collect it at all.

Who we share it with

We share consumer health data only with processors that handle it for us, under contract, to provide the feature you turned on:

  • Cloudflare, which runs our server that passes AI reading text on (it keeps no logs);
  • Google (Gemini API, paid service), which reads the text;
  • PostHog (US region), which stores beta usage counts.

We have no affiliates that receive this data. We don't share it with anyone else, and we don't sell it. Apple stores your iCloud data and Health data on your behalf under your agreement with Apple; we have no access to either.

Your rights

Whatever state you live in, you can:

  • confirm whether we collect or share your consumer health data, and get a list of everyone we shared it with;
  • get a copy of it;
  • have it deleted, including by our processors;
  • withdraw your consent: turn off AI reading in Settings → AI, and beta usage counts in Settings → Privacy. Turning usage counts off deletes your random install ID and any waiting events on the phone.

Email shaharechaitanya3@gmail.com with "Health data request" in the subject. We confirm your request and reply within 45 days, and tell you if we need up to 45 more days. Because usage counts carry only a random ID, we can find them only if you send us that ID. We don't charge for requests and won't treat you differently for making one.

Appeals

If we turn down your request, you can appeal by replying to our answer with "Appeal" in the subject. We'll answer the appeal in writing within 45 days and explain our reasons. If you're not satisfied, you can contact the Washington State Attorney General.

Changes

We'll update the date above when this page changes, and ask for your consent again before collecting or sharing consumer health data in a new way.
